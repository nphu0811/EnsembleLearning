# Craft: how a motion deck looks designed

Read this before Phase 3 and again before every review. It is what separates a deck people remember
from a deck that passed its checks. Numbers come from the accepted ca trù deck (`reference-bar.md`)
and from the five style packs, which encode them.

Contents: 1 The set · 2 Signature · 3 Canvas and grid · 4 Type · 5 Colour · 6 Composition library ·
7 Words on screen · 8 Imagery · 9 Depth and detail · 10 Data · 11 Anti-patterns · 12 The critic's pass

## 1. The set (what is the same on every slide)

A motion deck is a short film shot on one set. The audience sees 30-50 frames in ten minutes; what
stays the same is what makes them read as one piece, and what makes the changes visible.

| Layer | What | In the reference | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground | a material, never a flat fill | dark lacquer with brush streaks and vignette; red crackle lacquer for the turn | style `grounds` (generated by `make_assets.py ground`); a second ground for the colour arc |
| Chrome | the frame of the film | gold hairline frame inset 0.28 in at 65%, page number bottom right | style `chrome` (`!!frame`, `!!pageno`), applied by the engine on every slide |
| Signature | one object that belongs to the subject | three strings and a peg in the left margin, 48/48 slides | `!!` layers you design (section 2) |
| Type system | the same roles at the same sizes | kicker 13 pt tracked caps, title 54 pt serif, body 16 pt | style `type` roles; use `role`, not sizes |
| Grid | the same margins | text starts at x 1.45 in on every slide | style `grid`; place with `col` |

Keep the set fixed and change the composition. That is the whole trick: Morph can only show change
against things that stay still.

## 2. Signature (the object that carries the deck)

Pick ONE recurring object whose shape belongs to the subject, small enough to live in a margin and
strong enough to become the hero at two or three moments.

- It rests in the chrome or the margin on most slides (the strings at x 0.8-1.06 in).
- It becomes the hero at the hook, the turn and the close (one string in darkness → the instrument
  assembles → the strings lie down into a timeline → the string returns in the close).
- It is drawn in the same finish as the rest of the deck (gold foil, ink line, flat colour).
- It is several `!!` shapes, so Morph can move its parts (three lines + a peg; head/neck/body).

Ideas by kind (more in `genres.md`): heritage, the instrument's strings or a medallion; product,
the cursor, the app icon, a light beam; data, the headline number, one line that becomes every
chart's axis; education, a pencil line or a chalk dot; event, a ticket stub or a stage light.

A signature is not a logo in the corner. It moves, it means something, and it pays off at the end.

## 3. Canvas, grid and safe area

- 16:9, 13.333 × 7.5 in. Never 4:3.
- Safe area: keep text 0.45 in from every edge (the engine warns; the audit warns under 0.3 in).
- 12 columns with a 0.25 in gutter between the style's left and right margins; place blocks with
  `"col": [start, span]`. Text columns are 5-7 columns wide; a full-width title spans 10-12.
- Align to a few verticals. In the reference every kicker starts at x 1.45 in and titles at 1.35 in
  (the serif's overhang is optically aligned).
- Parking: an object waiting off-slide sits at x ≥ 13.6 in or y ≤ -2 in so it can fly in on the
  next keyframe; the audits ignore parked shapes.

## 4. Type

Two families, three at most (a display face, a text face, an optional mono or label face), static
TTFs with full coverage of the on-slide language (`fetch_fonts.py`, `check_fonts.py --lang vi`).

| Role | Size (pt) | Line | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| kicker | 11-13 bold | 1.0 | caps, tracked +2 to +5 pt, accent colour; names the section |
| display | 80-110 | 0.85-0.92 | 1-3 words; the hook, the product name, the close |
| numeral | 120-180 | 0.85 | one number as the hero |
| title | 40-56 | 0.88-1.0 | the slide's conclusion, ≤ 8 words, one line when possible |
| subtitle | 20-26 | 1.0-1.25 | italic serif or a light sans; one sentence |
| body | 15-17 | 1.25-1.42 | ≤ 60 characters per line, ≤ 3 lines per block |
| cardtitle | 22-30 | 0.95-1.05 | inside cards, timelines, bars |
| label / caption | 9-11 | 1.0 | credits, sources, axis labels; never content |

- Use the style's roles (`"role": "title"`). A deck that invents sizes per slide ends with 30 sizes
  (the reference has 31; the audit warns over 12).
- Vietnamese: stacked marks (ẫ ỡ ự Ỹ) need a face that draws them (`check_fonts.py`), and display
  lines at 0.85-0.9 still clear them in PowerPoint. Check the render at 4× on the tallest line.
- No single word alone on the last line of a title (`build_deck.py` warns); rewrite or widen.
- Tracking: + for small caps labels, − (−0.5 to −2 pt) for big grotesk display, 0 for body.
- Never: outline text, WordArt, shadowed text, centred paragraphs of body text, all-caps sentences.

## 5. Colour

| Role | Share of the frame | Example (lacquer-heritage) |
|---|---|---|
| ground | ~60% | #140B08 lacquer |
| surface / ground2 | ~25% | #24150F cards; #5E1410 the red ground of the turn |
| ink, head, muted, faint | ~10% | cream text, gold-cream titles, muted labels, hairlines |
| accent (+ accent2) | ≤ 5% | gold: the one thing to look at |

- One lit accent per frame: the part the audience should watch (the lit card, the active node,
  the highlighted bar). Everything else is head, ink or muted.
- Contrast: body 4.5:1 against what is really behind it, large text 3:1 (`check_deck.py --renders`
  measures it). Quiet text (dim cards, inactive nodes) stays ≥ 4.5:1 for anything you expect read.
- Text over photos: a veil (45-70%) or a gradient scrim behind the text, never text straight on detail.
- Colour arc: plan where colour arrives. Muted (sepia, duotone, veil) for the problem or the past; a
  second ground or a bloom (a 2 s Morph) at the turn; full colour after. Write it as
  `S1-S4 #0E0A07 → S6-S11 #F1E4C8`.
- Use colour as information only when colour is the subject (RGB channels, brand colours, a heat map).

## 6. Composition library

Within a scene keep one composition (keyframes change positions, not layouts). Across scenes vary the
composition inside the same system. These compositions cover almost every scene:

| Composition | Use for | Keyframe recipe (motion-grammar.md) |
|---|---|---|
| Void: one object in darkness, ≤ 6 words | the hook, a pause, the anchor phrase | Assemble |
| Hero object: object right third, heading left | title, product name, the reveal | Assemble, Colour bloom |
| Split 5/7: text column + visual column | explanations, a place, a feature | Push-in, Zoom to detail |
| Full-bleed photo + veil + heading bottom-left | places, people, the atmosphere | Push-in |
| Big numeral + one line | the problem, a proof, a turn | Counter |
| Highlight walk: three cards, one lit | roles, features, reasons, options | Highlight walk |
| Rail: nodes on a line, one active | history, a process, a roadmap, a video's chapters | Timeline walk |
| Screen in context: device + heading + steps | product, demo | Screen tour |
| Chart grow: bars or a line that build | growth, comparison | Bar grow |
| List reveal + one supporting number | reasons, steps (≤ 3 visible) | Reveal |
| Quote: hanging mark, large italic, attribution | a customer, an expert, the anchor | Hold |
| Framed media inside an object (book, screen, frame) | chapters, video frames, team material | Book |
| Echo of the opening composition | the close | Circular close |

Rules: one focal point per frame; the focal point sits on a third, not dead centre (except Void
and Quote); leave at least a third of the frame empty; align every block to the grid; repeat a
composition in another scene only when the repetition means something (the close echoing the hook).

## 7. Words on screen

The speaker carries the sentences; the slide carries the proof and the image.

- Title ≤ 8 words, stating the conclusion ("Tháng 7 là bước ngoặt", not "Doanh thu theo tháng").
- Body ≤ 30 words per keyframe, ≤ 3 lines per block; list ≤ 3 items visible.
- Every number carries its unit and its source (a source line in the chrome, a caption, or the notes).
- Fact IDs stay off the slide: they trace in the storyboard's `source` column and in the notes.
- An anchor phrase (5-8 words) appears on 3-4 slides: setup, middle, end.

## 8. Imagery

Order of preference: real (photos, screenshots, scans) → drawn from the TRUE list → nothing.
Never stock icons, clip art, emoji, generic gradients, or AI pictures of the subject itself.

- Photos are treated into the palette: duotone or sepia for the past, a veil for text, full colour
  for the bloom (`make_assets.py photo --mode duotone --pair`). Crop to the composition (`fit: cover`
  with a `focus`); never stretch.
- Drawn objects share one finish (gold foil, ink, chalk, flat colour: `make_assets.py foil`) and one
  line weight. Count the parts against `accuracy.md` (strings, pegs, beaters).
- Objects that move are split into parts on one shared canvas (`make_assets.py layers`), placed with
  the same box, so Morph can assemble, explode and rotate them.
- Screens sit in a device or a framed panel (`screen` component) with a shadow, a version label when
  real, an "illustration" label when drawn.

## 9. Depth and detail

- Rich at 1×, rewarding at 4×: every region has something true to find (a ruler of ticks on a rail,
  a seam on a card, a woven band, a page number, a credit line). Empty is fine; unfinished is not.
- Detail belongs to something: a rule under a card title, a dot at the end of a line, a band at the
  bottom of a ground. Lines end on an object, a dot or an edge, never in the air.
- Shadows only for lifted things (screens, lit cards, paper), from one light direction (top), soft
  (blur 15-30 pt, distance 4-10 pt, 10-55% depending on the ground). studio-pop's hard offset shadow
  is the one deliberate exception and is the same on every card.
- No bevels, 3D presets, glows on text, reflections, or shadows on hairlines.

## 10. Data

- One message per chart: the title says it, the highlight shows it (one accent bar, the rest muted).
- Bars grow from the baseline over a keyframe pair (`bars` with `grow` 0 → 1); values appear when
  the bars land; then a third keyframe moves the highlight.
- A big number gets its unit, its period and its comparison ("+48% · quý 3 so với quý 2").
- Illustrative numbers say so on the slide (a source line). Real numbers name their source.
- No 3D charts, no pie charts with more than 3 slices, no gridlines heavier than a hairline.

## 11. Anti-patterns (each one seen in a delivered deck)

| Seen | Why it fails | Instead |
|---|---|---|
| One screenshot stretched across a scene, only the words morph | nothing moves; the audience stops looking | push in on it, or bring a second visual; hold at most one beat per scene |
| One text-left/image-right template on every scene | 12 slides that look like one | the composition library, inside one system |
| Default navy theme, Calibri, flat fills | reads as a template | a style pack, a material ground, two real typefaces |
| Generic national symbols (Đông Sơn drum for ca trù) | wrong, and experts notice | the TRUE list in `accuracy.md` |
| Fact IDs and citations printed on every slide | clutter; looks like a paper | sources in the storyboard, notes and credits slide |
| Text over busy photo detail | unreadable on a projector | veil, scrim, or move the text |
| Cards all lit, all the same weight | no focal point | one lit, the others quiet (dim at 66%) |
| Every element entering with an animation | the talk waits for the slide | entrances only for new information, ≤ 3 per slide |
| Words that wrap one per line in a narrow box | looks broken | measure (`typeset.py`), widen, shorten |
| Transparent quiet text in LibreOffice renders | Vietnamese marks drift | quiet text as a mixed solid colour (`fade`) |

## 12. The critic's pass (before you call a group done)

1. Open the contact sheet. Does it read as ONE deck (ground, chrome, signature, type) at thumbnail size?
2. `studio.py compare WORK renders/<group>-N`: put it above the reference. Is it as rich? as calm? as specific?
3. Open every changed slide at full size. Then crop 4× on: the tallest title (diacritics), the
   smallest text, every place two things meet (a title and the cards below it, a label and a bar),
   every line end, every image edge.
4. Read the audit: zero ERROR; every WARN either fixed or written down as deliberate in `notes/<group>.md`.
5. Ask: is anything flat, generic, crowded, or unexplained? Fix it now, not after 40 slides.
